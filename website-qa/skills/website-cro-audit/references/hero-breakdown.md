# Landing Page Hero Section — Detailed Breakdown

> Companion deep-dive to the CRO brief. The hero is the screenful a visitor sees
> before scrolling. It carries roughly **80% of the conversion impact**, because
> most people decide whether to stay within about five seconds. Everything below
> the fold only matters if the hero earns the scroll. This document breaks the
> hero into its parts, defines what good looks like for each, and ends with a
> spec sheet and pre-launch checklist.

## A. The job of the hero

In the first five seconds the hero must answer three questions, in order. If any
answer is missing or unclear, the visitor leaves.

1. **What is this?** — the product or offer, in plain language an outsider understands.
2. **Is it for me?** — the audience and the problem it solves are obvious.
3. **What do I do next?** — one clear, obvious action.

**The 5-second test:** show the hero to someone unfamiliar with the product for
five seconds, hide it, and ask what the company does and what they'd click. If
they can't answer, the hero fails — no matter how beautiful it is.

## B. The components

A strong hero is built from up to seven parts. Not all are mandatory; the
**headline, primary CTA, and visual** are.

### 1. Eyebrow / pre-headline *(optional)*
- **Purpose:** Sets category or audience context before the headline.
- **Spec:** 2–5 words, often uppercase or in the accent colour, smaller than the headline.
- **Use it when:** It adds real context (audience, category, campaign). Otherwise omit.
- ✓ `FOR B2B FINANCE TEAMS`  ✗ `WELCOME TO OUR WEBSITE`

### 2. Headline *(the single most important element)*
- **Job:** Communicate the core value in plain language. Clarity beats cleverness.
- **Length:** Roughly 6–12 words. One idea, not three.
- **Rules:** Specific over vague; benefit over feature; no internal jargon; readable by someone who's never heard of you.
- **Reliable formulas:**
  - Outcome without pain — "[Desired outcome] without [the usual pain]."
  - Value proposition — "[Product] helps [audience] [do X] so they can [benefit]."
  - Category claim — "The [category] built for [specific audience]."
- ✓ `Close your monthly books in a day, not a week`
- ✓ `The scheduling tool built for busy dental clinics`
- ✗ `Reimagine the future of productivity`  ✗ `Welcome — we're passionate about innovation`

### 3. Subheadline / supporting line
- **Job:** Expand the headline — add the how, the proof, or the key differentiator. Never just restate the headline.
- **Length:** 1–2 sentences, roughly 15–30 words.
- ✓ `Automated reconciliation pulls every transaction in real time, so your numbers are always ready for review.`
- ✗ `We are a leading, world-class, innovative solution for all your needs.`

### 4. Primary CTA *(the button)*
- **Copy:** First-person and value-based: action verb + what they get. "Start my free trial," not "Submit."
- **Count:** Exactly one primary action. Everything else is visually subordinate.
- **Size & contrast:** Large (≥ 44px tall), and the single highest-contrast element in the hero.
- **Placement:** Directly beneath the value text and above the fold.
- **Microcopy:** A short risk-reducer underneath: "No card required · 2-minute setup."

### 5. Secondary CTA *(optional)*
- **Job:** A lower-commitment path for the not-yet-ready — "See how it works," "Watch a 2-min demo."
- **Treatment:** Text link or ghost/outline button. Must look clearly subordinate.

### 6. Hero visual
- **Job:** Show the product or outcome, reinforce the message, draw the eye toward the CTA.
- **Pick the right type:**
  - **Product / UI screenshot** — best for software — show the actual interface, ideally the "after / success" state.
  - **Hero product shot** — physical/e-commerce — clean, high-quality, single focal point.
  - **Lifestyle / outcome image** — services — show the result or the relieved customer.
  - **Short explainer / looping video** — powerful if it loads fast and plays muted; never autoplay with sound.
  - **Illustration** — only when it clarifies the concept — not decorative filler.
- **Rules:** Real and specific over abstract/stock. One focal point. If a person is shown, their gaze should point toward the headline or CTA. Never let the image push the CTA below the fold. Optimise file size.
- ✗ Generic stock handshake, abstract 3D blobs, or a smiling team that says nothing about the product.

### 7. Trust strip / social-proof bar
- **Job:** Instant credibility exactly where the decision is being made.
- **Forms:** Customer logos ("Trusted by…"), star rating + review count, a headline metric ("10,000+ teams"), a one-line testimonial, or security/award badges.
- **Rule:** Keep it light, scannable, real, and specific. Vague or invented proof does more harm than none.

## C. Layout & visual hierarchy

Three patterns cover almost every hero:
- **Split (text left, visual right)** — the default for SaaS and most products.
- **Centred (text centred, visual below)** — clean and focused for simple offers / single actions.
- **Text-over-image (full-bleed background)** — striking but risky — protect legibility/contrast with overlays.

Whatever the pattern, the eye should move: **eyebrow → headline → subheadline →
primary CTA → trust**, with the visual supporting rather than stealing attention.
Make that order obvious through size, weight, colour, and whitespace. Give the
headline and CTA room to breathe. The CTA should be the highest-contrast element
on screen; any text over an image must clear normal contrast ratios.

## D. The mobile hero

Most visitors arrive on a phone — design the mobile hero deliberately. The split
layout collapses into a single stacked column, and the stacking order is a real
decision.

- **MOBILE — Stacking order:** headline → subheadline → compact visual → primary CTA → trust is the safe default; lead with the visual only if it sells faster. Worth A/B testing.
- **MOBILE — Keep the hero short:** headline, sub, and primary CTA (or a clear scroll cue) reachable without a long scroll.
- **MOBILE — Type sizes:** headline bold and prominent (~28–32px+); body text ≥ 16px so nothing requires zooming.
- **MOBILE — CTA placement:** full-width or within the thumb zone; a sticky CTA bar is strong on long pages.
- **MOBILE — Visual handling:** crop or zoom for small screens — don't just shrink a wide desktop image into illegibility.
- **MOBILE — No hover-dependent reveals:** touch has no hover, so nothing essential hides behind it.

## E. Message match & personalisation

The headline must mirror the promise of whatever brought the visitor — the ad,
email, or link they clicked. A mismatch between the ad's wording and the hero is
one of the fastest ways to lose a paid click. If several campaigns point at the
same page, consider swapping the headline (and visual) dynamically per source.

## F. Common hero mistakes

- Clever but vague headline that never says what you actually do.
- Multiple competing CTAs splitting attention.
- Meaningless stock imagery that adds nothing.
- CTA below the fold or low-contrast — the action must be impossible to miss.
- A wall of text instead of a tight headline + subheadline.
- No social proof near the point of decision.
- Auto-rotating carousels / sliders — visitors miss most slides; they hurt conversion.
- Autoplay video with sound — an instant bounce trigger.

## G. Hero spec sheet

| Element        | Recommended spec               | Notes                              |
| -------------- | ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- |
| Eyebrow        | 2–5 words (optional)           | Uppercase or accent colour; small  |
| Headline       | 6–12 words, one idea           | Largest text; clarity first        |
| Subheadline    | 15–30 words, 1–2 sentences     | Adds new info, not a restate       |
| Primary CTA    | 2–5 words, ≥ 44px tall         | Highest contrast; value verb       |
| CTA microcopy  | Under ~8 words                 | Risk reducer beneath button        |
| Secondary CTA  | Text link / ghost button       | Visually subordinate               |
| Hero visual    | Shows product or outcome       | One focal point; optimised file    |
| Trust strip    | 3–6 logos or rating + count    | Real and specific                  |
| Above the fold | H1 + sub + CTA visible         | Test ~360–414px and ~1280px wide   |

## H. Pre-launch QA checklist

- [ ] Passes the 5-second test — stranger can name what you do and what to click.
- [ ] Message match verified — against each traffic source pointing at the page.
- [ ] Exactly one primary CTA — visible, high-contrast, value-based copy.
- [ ] **MOBILE** — Value text + CTA above the fold — confirmed on mobile and desktop.
- [ ] Visual reinforces the message — not generic or decorative.
- [ ] Social proof present — near the CTA.
- [ ] **MOBILE** — Contrast & legibility checked — text and button meet contrast ratios; body ≥ 16px.
- [ ] Hero loads fast — under ~2.5s, including the visual.
