## 3. COMPETITOR ANALYSIS (12 LOCAL COMPETITORS)

### Market Overview
Scottsdale is one of the most saturated and review-driven med spa markets in the Southwest US. There are **25+ med spas with 50+ Google reviews** operating locally, with new entrants every quarter. The market is affluent, image-conscious, and dominated by word-of-mouth and Instagram content. Price competition exists at the entry level, but the premium tier is under-branded — most high-end spas lean on the same generic language ("natural results," "personalized care").

## 4. MARKET INSIGHTS

### Target Audience / ICP

**Primary ICP: "The Scottsdale Glow Seeker"**
- **Age:** 35-55 (core), secondary 25-34 (preventive Botox growing fast)
- **Gender:** 80-85% female; male segment growing but secondary
- **Income:** Household income $100K-$200K+ (nearly 50% of Scottsdale HHs earn $100K+)
- **Lifestyle:** Health-conscious, image-aware, active social life, values experiences over things, follows aesthetic influencers on Instagram/TikTok, already spending on beauty (salon, skincare, fitness)
- **Mindset:** Wants to look like herself — refreshed, not different. Fear: being overdone or looking "fake." Desire: natural results that make friends ask "what are you doing differently?"
- **Buying triggers:** Friend referral > Instagram discovery > Google search. Converts when: she sees real results from a provider she trusts, feels no pressure, and has an easy booking path.

**Secondary ICP: "The Scottsdale Newcomer" (ages 28-38)**
- Considering Botox/preventive treatments for the first time
- Anxious about pain, looking unnatural, cost
- Needs education + reassurance + entry-point offer

### Top Services by Demand Priority
1. **Neuromodulators (Botox/Dysport)** — highest volume, lowest barrier; entry point for new clients
2. **Lip fillers** — high social media demand, Instagram-visual, aspirational
3. **Cheek/midface fillers** — high-value procedure, premium ticket
4. **Medical-grade facials** (HydraFacial, BioRePeel) — recurring revenue, lower risk perception
5. **Laser treatments** (BBL, IPL, skin resurfacing) — seasonal (fall/winter peak)
6. **Body contouring** (Emsculpt, CoolSculpting) — higher purchase price, longer decision cycle

### Seasonality (Scottsdale-Specific)
- **Peak 1: September–November** — post-summer skin recovery (sun damage, hyperpigmentation from AZ heat). Scottsdale gets an influx of seasonal residents ("snowbirds") from October. Laser treatments, BBL, IPL peak here.
- **Peak 2: January–March** — snowbird season fully in swing, social event calendar picks up, Valentine's Day promos work well. Filler and Botox demand high.
- **Moderate: April–May** — pre-summer bookings; skin tightening and body contouring appointments before swimsuit season.
- **Slower: June–August** — extreme heat keeps some clients home; outdoor events reduced. Focus on indoor wellness, facials, injectable maintenance. Good time for acquisition campaigns targeting new clients during slower competitor cycles.

### Key Customer Pains / Objections
1. "I don't want to look fake or overdone." → Counter: artistry + natural results proof
2. "I don't know which treatment I actually need." → Counter: educational content + consultation promise
3. "Will it hurt? What's the downtime?" → Counter: comfort protocol details + realistic expectation content
4. "Med spas here push you to spend more than you planned." → Counter: no-pressure promise, transparent pricing
5. "How do I know the injector is actually good?" → Counter: named practitioners + credentials + reviews
6. "It's expensive. Is it worth it?" → Counter: outcome-first storytelling, financing options, value-per-result framing

### Buying Triggers
- Seeing a specific result on a friend's face → drives referral
- Instagram/TikTok before/after content from a local provider
- "Special event upcoming" — wedding, reunion, vacation
- Turning 35/40/45 — milestone birthdays trigger first-timer decisions
- Seasonal refresh desire (post-summer, pre-holiday)

### Trust Factors That Matter Most
1. Named, photographed practitioners with credentials
2. Before/after photos (real, unfiltered, local clients)
3. Google/Yelp rating + review count (visible, not buried)
4. Professional but warm tone — not salesy
5. Clear pricing or transparent consultation process
6. Location/facility photos (clean, premium, inviting)

### Proof Opportunities for Lumière
- "X clients served in Scottsdale" (milestone number)
- Before/after gallery by treatment (filterable)
- Video testimonials from real clients
- Practitioner spotlight content (training, philosophy, technique)
- Seasonal content showing real Scottsdale clients' results

## 7. CREATIVES DIRECTION

### Research Finding: What Wins in This Niche
- Before/after content is the #1 conversion driver for med spa ads (highest CTR + lowest CPL)
- Short-form video gets 2-3x engagement vs. static on Instagram/Meta
- UGC-style (authentic, phone-shot) often outperforms polished studio work for trust
- First 3 seconds are the entire hook — the overlay text must carry the emotional punch
- "Show the result, tease the transformation" structure wins
- Meta/Instagram are primary channels for Scottsdale's 35-55 female demographic

### Visual Quality Standard
- **Premium editorial aesthetic** — think luxury skincare brand quality, not typical med spa stock
- **Palette match to website:** Champagne cream / Deep noir / Luminescent gold / Blush mauve
- **Faces as art** — close-up skin texture, glowing highlight, neutral expressions showing confidence
- **Location signal** where possible — Scottsdale luxury context (desert modernism, white architecture, warm light)
- **NO generic stock** — all visuals should feel like they could be on a Scottsdale woman's real Instagram feed

## 9. FINAL RECOMMENDATION

### Strongest Direction: Variation A ("The Artistry Page") as the primary website, with Variation B as the paid acquisition landing page for first-timer campaigns.

**Why Variation A wins:**
- Scottsdale's market is saturated with competent-but-generic providers. The artistry angle + French brand name gives Lumière a positioning moat no competitor currently holds.
- "Artistry-first" speaks directly to the primary ICP's fear (looking overdone) and desire (looking authentically better).
- The color system (champagne/noir/gold) creates a visually distinctive brand the creatives team can build a cohesive ad world around.
- The named-practitioner architecture is the trust mechanism that converts the research-phase buyer.

**What to test first:**
1. A/B test Variation A (artistry) vs. Variation B (first-timer) as paid landing pages — the first-timer angle may outperform in cold traffic campaigns targeting 28-38 females.
2. Creative priority: Lead with Creative 2 (natural before/after) and Creative 3 (first-timer video) in the first 30 days. These will gather the fastest learnings.
3. SEO anchors: "med spa Scottsdale," "Botox Scottsdale," "natural results Scottsdale med spa," "medical grade facial Scottsdale"

**Most important positioning decision:**
Name-driven brand identity — the Lumière name only works if the visual execution matches the French-elegance implied. A generic layout will waste the name. The website must feel distinctively premium or the brand promise rings hollow.

**Risk to watch:**
If the Lumière team's practitioner credentials don't yet include specific named injectors with verifiable backgrounds, Hook 4 (Injector Credibility) and Hook 7 (Trusted Scottsdale Injector) can't be fully activated. Prioritize getting practitioner bios + photography before launch.
