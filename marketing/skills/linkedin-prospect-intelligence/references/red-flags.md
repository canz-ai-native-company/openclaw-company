# Red Flags & Disqualifiers

Load this when running Step 5 of the workflow.

A high ICP fit score doesn't matter if there's a critical red flag. This file catches the prospects who *look* good on paper but will waste the user's time or actively damage the relationship if pursued. Better to skip 10 marginal prospects than to send the wrong message to one bad-fit person and lose the chance forever.

## How to Use

After ICP scoring (Step 4), run through this checklist. Any "Yes" on a critical flag means **recommend skip** — even if the ICP score is high.

For non-critical flags, document them but let the user decide.

## Critical Red Flags (Recommend Skip)

### 1. Recent Layoff / Job Loss / Forced Exit

**Signals:**
- "Open to work" banner
- Recent post about leaving a role unexpectedly
- "Excited for what's next" posts following a tenure that ended abruptly
- Public layoff at their company recently

**Why it's a red flag:** They don't have buying authority, even if their title still shows it. Outreach is also tone-deaf.

**What to do:** Skip outreach now. Note them for re-engagement when their next role is announced (and they'll often be open to new tools at the new role).

---

### 2. Company Contraction / Financial Distress

**Signals:**
- Recent layoffs at the company (public news)
- Posts mentioning "tough quarter", "right-sizing", "doing more with less" in a defensive tone
- Funding round didn't happen as expected (public reporting)
- Hiring freeze announcements
- Office closures

**Why it's a red flag:** Budgets are frozen. Even strong-fit prospects can't buy.

**What to do:** Mark as nurture-only. Don't pitch. Re-evaluate in 3-6 months.

---

### 3. Visible Competitor Relationship

**Signals:**
- They've publicly praised, endorsed, or are using a direct competitor to the user's offer
- Recent case study with a competitor
- Co-authored content with a competitor
- LinkedIn recommendations from competitor leadership

**Why it's a red flag:** Displacement sales are 10x harder. Don't waste a cold approach on this — needs a different play entirely.

**What to do:** Mark as "long-cycle nurture only" or skip. If pursuing, the outreach angle must be completely different (no comparison, no direct pitch — only nurture until something changes).

---

### 4. Audience Mismatch

**Signals:**
- The prospect's role/title looks right but their actual content reveals they speak to a totally different audience than the user's offer serves
- Example: title says "Founder, SaaS" but content reveals it's a one-person consulting practice — wrong company stage entirely

**Why it's a red flag:** ICP scoring missed the nuance. The titled role lied; the content told the truth.

**What to do:** Re-score against actual ICP. If still misfit, skip.

---

### 5. Active Hostility / Cynicism Toward the Category

**Signals:**
- Posts explicitly criticizing AI, automation, or whatever category the user's offer falls into
- "All this AI hype is nonsense" type content
- Mocking outbound, cold outreach, sales tooling
- "We're going back to fundamentals" away from the category

**Why it's a red flag:** Any cold outreach about the category will be received as proof of their criticism.

**What to do:** Skip cold outreach. Consider only warm intros, and only if their public position has visibly softened.

---

### 6. Legal / PR Trouble

**Signals:**
- Recent public scandal, lawsuit, investigation
- Significant negative press in the last 30-60 days
- Founder/exec accused of something serious

**Why it's a red flag:** They're in crisis mode, not buying mode. Also reputational risk for the user to be visibly associated.

**What to do:** Skip until the situation resolves and they're publicly out of crisis mode.

---

## Non-Critical Red Flags (Document, Let User Decide)

### 7. Tone / Values Incompatibility

**Signals:**
- Their public content tone is incompatible with the user's brand (e.g., user's brand is thoughtful/measured, prospect is aggressive/braggy)
- Values mismatch (user's brand emphasizes craft, prospect celebrates shortcuts/hacks)

**Why it matters:** Even if you close them, they'll be a bad reference, a tough customer, or churn fast.

**What to do:** Note it. Some users are willing to take all revenue; others filter for fit.

---

### 8. Inactive Profile

**Signals:**
- No posts in 6+ months
- Last activity is generic likes, no original content
- Profile feels stale (no role updates, no engagement)

**Why it matters:** Hard to personalize without content signals. Reply rates will be lower because they don't actively check LinkedIn.

**What to do:** Note it. May need a different channel (email, mutual intro) instead of LinkedIn DM.

---

### 9. Title-Role Mismatch

**Signals:**
- Title is impressive but content reveals they're a junior individual contributor in a large org
- "Founder" but no company is actually operating (parked side project)

**Why it matters:** Authority score in ICP is inflated.

**What to do:** Re-score authority. Adjust outreach angle from "decision-maker" to "champion-building".

---

### 10. Highly Public Sales Aversion

**Signals:**
- Recent posts complaining about cold outreach in general
- "Stop pitching me on LinkedIn" type content
- Public lists of "bad outreach examples"

**Why it matters:** Any first DM is high-risk. Comment-first is mandatory.

**What to do:** Use the Comment-First path. Build the relationship through engagement before any DM. Never pitch in early messages — make sure first 2-3 touches are pure value-add.

---


## Decision Logic

```
If any critical red flag (1-6) is "Yes" → recommend SKIP with reason
If only non-critical red flags (7-10) are "Yes" → document and let user decide, adjust outreach approach
If no red flags → proceed with normal outreach recommendation
```

## Honesty Reminder

It is better to tell the user "skip this one" than to push them toward a prospect who will burn the relationship or waste their time. The skill earns trust by filtering, not by approving everyone.
