# Psychology Frameworks

Load this when running Step 3 of the workflow. Match the prospect to one (or hybrid of two) archetypes, then extract the message angle to use and the one to avoid.

Sales-relevant psychology is read from visible professional behavior only. Never infer religion, ethnicity, political affiliation, health, trauma, finances, or family. Stay in the lane of: language patterns, content topics, audience, what they celebrate vs criticize, tone, and decision-making style.

## The 7 Archetypes

### 1. Educational

**Detection signals:**
- Posts explain concepts, frameworks, "here's how X works"
- Uses analogies, breakdowns, step-by-step structure
- Audience is learners or peers in their field
- Celebrates clarity, depth, "first principles" thinking

**Motivational drivers:** mastery, teaching, intellectual leadership, building authority

**Trust triggers:** specificity, nuanced understanding of their domain, smart questions, references to their actual ideas

**Message angle to use:** Extend their idea. Add a perspective they haven't covered. Ask a thoughtful question that demonstrates you understood their argument.

**Message angle to avoid:** Generic praise ("great post!"), vague pitches, anything that signals you didn't read carefully.

---

### 2. Technical

**Detection signals:**
- Talks about systems, architecture, implementation, tradeoffs
- Uses domain-specific vocabulary (APIs, infra, models, pipelines, stack)
- Posts about builds, integrations, debugging stories
- Audience is other engineers/builders

**Motivational drivers:** elegance, leverage, system-level thinking, building things that work

**Trust triggers:** technical specificity, mention of actual tools/frameworks, evidence you understand their stack, no marketing fluff

**Message angle to use:** Talk about implementation. Reference a specific technical detail from their work. Lead with the system/architecture angle, not the business angle.

**Message angle to avoid:** ROI-heavy pitches, marketing language, vague capability claims, anything that sounds like a salesperson reading a script.

---

### 3. Founder / Operator

**Detection signals:**
- Posts about running the business, team, growth, hard decisions
- Mixes wins and pains openly
- Uses operator language: pipeline, runway, hiring, churn, cash, customers
- Audience is other founders, operators, investors

**Motivational drivers:** leverage, execution speed, team productivity, revenue growth, building something that lasts

**Trust triggers:** evidence you've done this before, specificity about their stage, no theory without practice, fast and direct communication

**Message angle to use:** Time savings, leverage, "this gives you back X hours/week", revenue impact, team productivity, removing a specific bottleneck.

**Message angle to avoid:** Long preambles, generic frameworks, anything that smells like a junior consultant pitch, hype.

---

### 4. Motivational / Thought-Leader

**Detection signals:**
- Posts about vision, transformation, "the future of X", movement-building
- Personal-brand-heavy (their name and face are central)
- Audience is community, followers, peers in their movement
- Celebrates transformation stories, big bets, conviction

**Motivational drivers:** influence, vision, community, leaving a mark, being right about the future

**Trust triggers:** alignment with their movement, ability to extend their vision, recognition of their leadership, no copying-but-not-crediting

**Message angle to use:** Vision alignment, "your point about X resonated because Y", positioning as a partner in their movement, not a vendor.

**Message angle to avoid:** Tactical-only pitches that ignore their bigger narrative, anything that reduces them to a transaction, contrarian challenges in a cold message.

---

### 5. Sales-Heavy

**Detection signals:**
- Posts about pipeline, conversion, deals closed, revenue, quotas
- Uses outbound/sales vocabulary (cadences, MQL, SQL, ICP, AE, SDR)
- Audience is other sales leaders, AEs, founders thinking about GTM
- Celebrates closed deals, hitting numbers, sales process improvements

**Motivational drivers:** revenue, speed, measurable outcomes, beating quota, building winning teams

**Trust triggers:** numbers, specificity about conversion lift, concrete pipeline impact, no fluff

**Message angle to use:** Direct outcome-led: "X clients increased reply rate by Y", "this cuts research time by Z minutes per lead". Speak their numbers language.

**Message angle to avoid:** Long stories, vague capability claims, anything that doesn't tie back to revenue or pipeline in the first 2 sentences.

---

### 6. Cautious / Compliance-Heavy

**Detection signals:**
- Posts about risk, governance, audit, security, reliability, regulation
- Speaks to enterprise, finance, healthcare, legal, regulated industries
- Celebrates rigor, process, "doing it right", not breaking things
- Audience is other risk-aware operators

**Motivational drivers:** risk reduction, reliability, audit-readiness, protecting the org

**Trust triggers:** evidence of safety, mention of human-in-the-loop, governance language, no overpromising, transparent limitations

**Message angle to use:** "Reduces risk by X", "with human approval gates", "auditable", "we don't replace, we augment". Lead with the safety angle.

**Message angle to avoid:** "AI does it all autonomously", "fully automated", "set and forget", anything that triggers their risk antennae.

---

### 7. Analytical

**Detection signals:**
- Posts with data, charts, breakdowns, "here's what the numbers show"
- Uses analytical vocabulary (cohorts, retention, LTV, attribution, segments)
- Often growth, marketing analytics, or product analytics roles
- Audience is other data-driven operators

**Motivational drivers:** truth, measurable outcomes, getting the model right, decisions backed by data

**Trust triggers:** data, benchmarks, "here's the methodology", specific metrics, willingness to show your work

**Message angle to use:** Lead with a number or a measurable claim. Offer to share methodology. "Based on N data points, X" beats "we help companies like yours."

**Message angle to avoid:** Vague claims, marketing speak without numbers, opinions presented as facts.

---

## Hybrid Archetypes

Real prospects often blend two archetypes. Common hybrids:

- **Founder + Educational** — Builds in public, shares lessons. Use a thoughtful extension of one of their lessons, then briefly tie to a business outcome.
- **Technical + Founder** — Engineering founder. Lead with the technical angle, close with the business outcome.
- **Sales-heavy + Motivational** — Sales leader with a vision. Mix outcome language with vision alignment.
- **Analytical + Compliance** — Risk-aware data lead. Lead with measurable safety/reliability claims.

When you detect a hybrid, name both archetypes and write a message that respects both lenses.

## What to Output for Step 3

Fill the "Personality & Professional Psychology" section of the report schema. Be specific. Don't write "communication style: professional" — that says nothing. Write something like "Direct, operator-flavored. Short posts, no fluff. Uses revenue/pipeline language. Frustrated tone toward outbound tooling specifically."

The whole point of this section is to drive message-writing in Step 8. If your psychology profile isn't specific enough to make Step 8 easy, it isn't done yet.

## Ethical Note

This is for respectful relevance, not manipulation. The goal is to write messages the prospect actually wants to receive — messages that meet them where they are. If you find yourself thinking about how to "exploit" or "trigger" something, stop. Reframe: how do I show up genuinely useful for someone with this professional psychology?
